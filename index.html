Python · Quantitative research

Trading Lab

A Python research engine built to test whether algorithmic cryptocurrency strategies remain credible under demanding validation.

01 / Research system

A workbench, not a scoreboard

The project is designed for developing and evaluating strategies across multiple timeframes. Its interface keeps normalized data, experiment metadata, and synchronized event evidence close together.

The emphasis is not simply on finding profitable historical results. Fees, data leakage, look-ahead bias, and execution assumptions remain visible so that weak ideas can be rejected early.

02 / Experiment archive

Every result has a place

Exploratory research and execution-aware backtests remain separate catalog entries. Each record connects a strategy to its dataset, research phase, signal count, clustering output, and completion state.

This structure makes comparison easier without allowing a later result to quietly replace an earlier artifact.

03 / Provenance

Evidence behind the experiment

Dataset lineage, validation status, stored counts, calculation versions, and run identifiers make each result inspectable. The detail view records what entered the pipeline and how far it progressed.

Walk-forward testing, Monte Carlo analysis, and structured metrics matter most when the inputs behind them can still be traced.

04 / Execution reality

The gap after costs

Gross and net results are shown together so the effect of fees and execution assumptions cannot disappear behind a headline return. The distance between the two is part of the strategy, not a footnote.

05 / Decision rules

How an idea leaves the market

Exit-rule comparisons expose how strategy behavior changes when the decision boundary moves. Testing those alternatives against the same interval helps separate a durable signal from a convenient parameter choice.

06 / Event context

One timeline, several views

Price, external fair value, mark, oracle, and stored signals are aligned around the same event window. Missing observations remain gaps instead of being silently connected or filled.

07 / Liquidations

Pressure made visible

Liquidation evidence adds context that price alone cannot provide. Bursts of forced activity can be inspected beside the same stored interval used by the strategy.

08 / Market state

The conditions around a signal

Liquidity state describes the environment in which a signal appears. That surrounding market structure helps distinguish similar price movements that occurred under very different conditions.

09 / Trade flow

From observation to judgment

Executed market activity completes the event view. Together, the synchronized panels turn a strategy result into something that can be inspected, challenged, and either advanced or discarded.

Roblox Studio · Luau

Naval Simulator

A real-time naval sandbox built around large ocean environments, detailed ships, and the systems that make a moving vessel feel inhabited.

01 / Ship handling

Weight on the water

The simulation begins with the relationship between the ship and the sea. Server-authoritative motion, tuned acceleration, and deliberate turning behavior give the vessel a sense of scale while keeping it responsive to the player.

Open-water runs and harbor approaches test the same system at different speeds, exposing how the hull settles, turns, and comes alongside the dock.

02 / Situational awareness

The bridge as an interface

The bridge connects first-person movement with the information needed to operate the ship. Radar contacts, range rings, headings, and target state are presented inside the world instead of separated into a conventional menu.

That approach keeps navigation and decision-making grounded in the vessel itself, whether the player is at a console or moving through the compartment.

03 / Weapons feedback

Following the launch

A dedicated camera follows the missile from launch through terminal guidance. The view makes the weapon system legible at speed and provides immediate feedback about tracking, trajectory, and impact.

It also creates a useful development tool: guidance behavior that is difficult to read from the ship becomes obvious when the camera travels with the projectile.

04 / Ship-relative movement

A moving place, not a moving prop

The ship is designed to be occupied while underway. Ship-relative character movement and collision handling allow players to cross the deck and move through modeled compartments without losing the motion of the larger vessel.

Exterior scale and interior detail are treated as one continuous environment, supporting a shift from third-person navigation to close first-person exploration.

05 / Sensor development

Making contact data readable

The radar view turns simulated contact data into an operator-facing picture. Range rings establish scale, heading marks preserve orientation, and trails reveal movement that a single blip cannot communicate.

Development overlays keep the underlying data visible while detection, identification, and display behavior are tuned together.

06 / Guidance systems

From contact to intercept

Missile guidance connects target acquisition, radar information, projectile state, and visible feedback. Each test run is an opportunity to compare what the system believes with what the projectile actually does.

The current footage captures that iterative stage, with diagnostic information left on-screen so tracking errors can be identified and corrected.

07 / Water effects

A wake that explains motion

Procedural bow and wake effects communicate speed, direction, and turning force before the player looks at an instrument. Diagnostic markers expose how those effects are positioned relative to the hull.

The system is tuned across straight runs and turns so spray and foam respond to the vessel instead of appearing as a fixed decoration.

08 / Ocean foundation

Building the horizon

An early tiled-ocean prototype established the project’s technical foundation. Level-of-detail geometry keeps the surrounding water broad enough for naval scale while concentrating detail near the player.

Wave shape, surface roughness, pitch, and roll were developed together, turning a flat test surface into an environment capable of carrying the rest of the simulation.

Blender · Animation · Visual development

Animation Projects

A growing collection of animation, motion, and visual-development studies made in Blender.

01 / Building the scene

Form before finish

The work begins with structure: broad forms, spatial relationships, and camera-readable silhouettes. This ship scene is still visibly inside the working viewport, where modeling and composition can evolve together.

These recordings are process material rather than polished reels. They preserve the decisions, revisions, and imperfect intermediate states that lead toward a finished shot.

02 / Motion study

Testing the beat

A short render is useful for checking timing without committing to a long sequence. Even a few frames can expose a pause that feels too long, a move that lands too softly, or a composition that stops reading once it is in motion.

This compact study sits alongside the larger development recordings as a quick checkpoint between viewport work and a more complete animation.

03 / Light and atmosphere

Finding the image

Lighting turns the model into a scene. The vehicle study explores how a restrained pool of light can reveal just enough form while allowing most of the frame to fall into darkness.

The goal at this stage is not final polish. It is to find the visual idea: where the eye should land, which details should disappear, and how the mood supports the motion.

04 / Staging

Character in context

The final recording brings character, environment, and camera together. Staging is treated as part of the animation itself: posture, framing, contrast, and surrounding geometry all influence how the moment is read.

Detailed production notes are still being prepared. For now, this continuous set of studies shows the work where it actually happens—between an initial blockout and the finished image.

Agricultural Drone Fleet

An autonomous aircraft research project exploring the future of coordinated agricultural operations, from field planning to simulated flight.

01 / Overview

A farm in motion

A live simulation brings aircraft, field activity, and decisions together, making a complex operation easy to observe as it evolves.

02 / Interface

Model, run, compare

Complex scenarios become clear, repeatable experiments. The interface is designed to make change visible and comparisons easy to understand.

03 / Environment

Build the field first

Configurable layouts make new ideas quick to explore, giving each experiment a controlled setting before it ever reaches a real field.

04 / Geography

From diagram to place

Map views connect each scenario to a real place, grounding the project in the scale and character of agricultural work.

05 / Results

Measure what matters

Comparisons reveal how small choices affect the whole system, turning each run into a concise story about tradeoffs and outcomes.

06 / Flight

From plan to flight

Flight simulation carries plans from dashboard to aircraft, showing how high-level ideas translate into motion inside a virtual environment.

07 / Vision

Toward a living farm

The larger vision is a living agricultural digital twin: an evolving view of the farm that helps autonomous tools support better decisions.
